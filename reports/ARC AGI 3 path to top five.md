# Kaggle-Only Compute Keeps Top Five Out of Reach

With Kaggle GPUs only, our team has no identified path from about 4% to the 16–19.45% top-five band before Nov 2. Our honest odds of a top-five final finish are about 1%. The realistic target is a clean 4.5–6% leaderboard showing plus a serious Paper Track entry, and the Paper Track is our best-value shot at prize money. Three facts drive this conclusion. First, the one base model the top teams almost certainly share is Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, and it cannot be fine-tuned on a single 96 GB RTX PRO 6000. The only competitor who reported training it used a full H200 node and got no gain ([discussion 742835](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/discussion/742835)). Second, every harness lever measured on our exact chassis (B81) is statistically indistinguishable from noise on the hidden set. The best of them is the B81 serving profile itself, worth roughly +0.5–0.7 points ([Thui MAP](https://github.com/Sahasawatt/arc-agi-3-agent/blob/master/notes/wayfinder/MAP.md)). Third, one hidden draw varies by about ±0.45 points at our level. With one submission per day, the 33 days left after Milestone #2 can test only one or two levers with enough power to trust the result. None of the top six teams has published a method. The largest single event we can exploit is therefore outside our control: Milestone #2 on Sep 30, when Tong Hui Kang (15.02%) may open-source his notebook ([discussion 742935](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/discussion/742935)). The fp8 KV-cache lead is real, but it is not a contradiction and not a breakthrough. Thuitanium's B86 failed on an older pinned vLLM build whose QSA attention backend accepts only bf16 KV. NVARC3 runs a patched vLLM fork carrying the fp8 port of PR #55557, since merged upstream. Its main effect is 1.77× more KV capacity, and extra KV has so far bought actions, not levels.

*How to read this report.* "Hidden" means the Kaggle leaderboard score, computed on 55 semi-private games. The final ranking uses the other 55 private games, which are played and scored during the same run. "Public-25" means offline scores on the 25 public demo games. Statements marked **[Inference]** are my reasoning from the cited sources, not sourced facts. All odds are my estimates.

## Fine-tuning Flash-Next needs a node Kaggle does not offer

Fine-tuning is **not feasible on Kaggle-only compute** for the model that matters, and not worth the quota for the model that fits. Flash-Next has 125B parameters (6B active) plus a 51B n-gram embedding table. It serves on the 96 GB card only because the routed experts are NVFP4 and the n-gram tables sit in host RAM ([Qwen3.8-Flash-Next card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next); [RadixArk NVFP4 config](https://huggingface.co/RadixArk/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-NVFP4)). On Kaggle the weights occupy 81.8 GiB of 94.4 GiB free ([Thui MAP B81](https://github.com/Sahasawatt/arc-agi-3-agent/blob/master/notes/wayfinder/MAP.md)), which leaves about 15–20 GB for LoRA weights, optimizer state, activations and fp32 logits. The software gap is worse than the memory gap. No trainer (Unsloth, PEFT with bitsandbytes, torchtune, ms-swift) supports backpropagating through frozen modelopt-NVFP4 experts on the `qwen4_exp` architecture. Unsloth advises against 4-bit QLoRA on MoE models and on the Qwen3.5 family at all ([Unsloth MoE](https://unsloth.ai/docs/basics/faster-moe); [Unsloth Qwen3.5](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/qwen3.5/fine-tune)). Participants concur. One says "rtx 6000 pro can not be used for training of any ~100B model even with aggressive cpu offload and quantization", after building a Flash-Next RL pipeline on a full H200 node that "did not gain any performance" ([742835](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/discussion/742835)). Another calls SFT on Flash-Next NVFP4 "nearly impossible with 96g RTX PRO 6000" ([732854](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/discussion/732854)). Merging an adapter back into the weights would mean re-quantizing a 250 GB bf16 model, which no Kaggle machine can hold.

The trainable alternative loses on arithmetic. A Qwen3.8-27B LoRA fits and has been run on Kaggle: Thuitanium's r16 adapter trained in about 7 hours on 1,826 winning turns. It made held-out games **worse**, scoring 2.45 / 3.69 against the base model's 4.61 / 6.35, and the arm was closed as null ([Sahasawatt/arc-agi-3-agent thui-lora](https://github.com/Sahasawatt/arc-agi-3-agent)). The best public positive result is a STaR LoRA that took a Qwen3.6-27B build from **1.25 to 1.94 hidden**, with the warning that "naively scaling the data hurt" ([739047](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/discussion/739047)). The 27B lineage scores about 1.2–1.7 hidden against about 4 for Flash-Next ([Thui LEDGER](https://github.com/Sahasawatt/arc-agi-3-agent)). To merely tie, a 27B fine-tune would need a 2.4–3.3× gain, and nothing in the evidence comes close. Quota compounds the problem. One account gets about **30 GPU-hours per week**, the RTX card "burns GPU quota faster" by an unpublished factor, and sessions cap at 9 hours ([734585](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/discussion/734585); [ARC-AGI-3-Kaggle-Starter](https://github.com/arcprize/ARC-AGI-3-Kaggle-Starter)). The Kaggle TPU v5e-8 has 128 GB of HBM, too little for Flash-Next in bf16, and there is no JAX training code for this architecture ([kaggle-tpu-lab](https://github.com/ARahim3/kaggle-tpu-lab)). Model swaps offer no escape either. Everything stronger on paper (GLM-5.3-Flash NVFP4 at 204 GB, MiMo-V2.6-Flash FP8 at 178 GB) overflows the card ([nvidia/GLM-5.3-Flash-NVFP4](https://hf.co/nvidia/GLM-5.3-Flash-NVFP4); [MiMo card](https://huggingface.co/XiaomiMiMo/MiMo-V2.6-Flash-RL)). Everything that fits (Gemma-4-31B, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, Qwen3.5-122B-A10B) has been measured far worse in this harness ([Thui MAP B94](https://github.com/Sahasawatt/arc-agi-3-agent/blob/master/notes/wayfinder/MAP.md)).

The decision is to **drop fine-tuning** from the Kaggle-only plan. One capped hedge remains defensible if spare quota appears in October: a zero-weight (B=0) LoRA serving smoke test on Flash-Next's bf16 attention modules, with the Duck `vllm_runtime_lora_guard` installed, killed if vLLM rejects or silently drops the adapter or if throughput falls more than 10% ([Tufalabs/duck-harness](https://github.com/Tufalabs/duck-harness)). It costs about 2 RTX-hours. Even a pass leaves the training blocker in place, so it belongs at the bottom of the queue.

## Only the serving profile has hidden evidence, and barely

Our submission is B81 plus three grafts: note backfill from hidden reasoning, a scoring-and-pacing prompt, and click candidates. Only the base has hidden support worth the name. Thuitanium recorded six hidden draws on the B81 profile (MTP off, KV cache 7 GiB, 28 sequences) with a mean of 3.92, against 3.21 for eight earlier Flash-Next draws. Across these heterogeneous builds the difference is +0.71, with a pooled permutation p ≈ 0.05 ([Thui MAP](https://github.com/Sahasawatt/arc-agi-3-agent/blob/master/notes/wayfinder/MAP.md)). tantan0327 cites four B81 draws of 4.50, 3.86, 3.03 and 5.36 against their own floor of 2.88 over 20 draws, and now submits a byte-identical B81 copy ([tantan SOLUTION.md](https://github.com/tantan0327/arc-agi-3-agent/blob/main/SOLUTION.md)). The grafts lean positive but prove nothing. Note backfill averaged 3.15 hidden against a 2.87 floor on an older vehicle, and its mechanism test failed: a placebo arm showed the same per-game delta ([tantan graft inventory](https://github.com/tantan0327/arc-agi-3-agent/blob/main/docs/graft-inventory.md); [Thui MAP B96](https://github.com/Sahasawatt/arc-agi-3-agent/blob/master/notes/wayfinder/MAP.md)). The prompt-plus-clicks bundle went **1.12 → 1.43** at n = 1 per arm on a weaker 27B model ([743060](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/discussion/743060)). sonpham-org meanwhile *removed* scoring-pressure text from their own harness, so even the sign of our pacing graft is contested ([sonpham trace finding](https://github.com/sonpham-org/arc-3/blob/main/docs/trace-findings/2026-09-21-no-score-pressure-wide.md)).

The null list is long and specific, which makes it useful. On Flash-Next, Thuitanium measured and closed as indistinguishable or worse: UNDO labelling (B76), no-impact detection (B80), 64k context (B88), dropping history at level transitions (B89, levels 41 → 35), a supervisor redirect (B90), prefix caching (B91), no longer re-sending stored reasoning (B92, levels 18 → 11), a model swap (B94), pinning cleared-level traces (B99) and stripping reasoning from finished levels (B100). In the 27B era, lowering reasoning effort to medium cut levels from 28 to 12 (p = 0.005 worse) ([Thui MAP](https://github.com/Sahasawatt/arc-agi-3-agent/blob/master/notes/wayfinder/MAP.md); [Thui LEDGER](https://github.com/Sahasawatt/arc-agi-3-agent)). The pattern matters more than any single row: history and stored reasoning are load-bearing, and every attempt to trim them lost. Time reallocation is priced small. Doubling every game's clock bought about 2 levels over 25 games, and Scott Le Grand's extra time for games at level 2+ raised public-25 from 7.6 to ~13 but was "a slight digression on the hidden data" ([740812](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/discussion/740812)). Local scores mislead, too: tantan measured a **Pearson r of −0.39** between offline and board scores ([tantan SOLUTION.md](https://github.com/tantan0327/arc-agi-3-agent/blob/main/SOLUTION.md)). No public kernel scores above 4.50 ([chiakazirim Duck Qwen3.8 Tuned](https://www.kaggle.com/code/chiakazirim/duck-qwen3-8-tuned)).

The statistics set the real limit on our plan. **[Inference]** With a same-build hidden sd of about 0.43–0.47, detecting a +0.5 effect at 80% power in a two-arm comparison needs about 13 draws per arm. Detecting +1.0 needs about 4 per arm. There are 39 UTC days from Sep 25 to Nov 2. Five of them go to Milestone #2, and a few are needed at the end to confirm finalists. So the whole remaining budget can settle **one medium-sized lever or three large ones**, and it cannot see anything smaller. That is why the plan below spends hidden draws only on changes that could plausibly move us a full point or more, and treats smaller tweaks as free passengers.

## Two vLLM builds explain the fp8 contradiction

The apparent conflict is simple: NVARC3 serves Flash-Next with an fp8 KV cache, while Thuitanium's B86 declared fp8 KV dead on the same model. **Both are true because they ran different vLLM code.** B86 ran on the pinned vLLM dev image that the B81 chassis ships (Keith Tyser's Flash-Next NVFP4 stack). In that build, the QSA (Qwen Sparse Attention) backend raises `NotImplementedError` unless the KV cache is bf16, and the checkpoint ships no k/v scales ([Thui MAP B86](https://github.com/Sahasawatt/arc-agi-3-agent/blob/master/notes/wayfinder/MAP.md)). PR #55557 exists precisely to add an fp8_e4m3 main KV cache on the QSA path for this architecture. Ivan Sorokin ported it onto his `qwen-flash-next` branch on Sep 7 under the title "[Model] Qwen4Exp: fp8_e4m3 main KV cache on the QSA path", and it merged upstream on **Sep 16** ([1ytic/vllm commit a4d0a70](https://github.com/1ytic/vllm/commit/a4d0a70693c12713873583c8edea1761909e4983); [PR #55557](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/55557)). On the RTX PRO 6000 the PR reports a KV pool of **~434K → ~772K tokens (1.77×)**, unchanged prefill, logprob deltas within noise, and a ~10% drop in MTP draft acceptance. The MTP penalty does not affect B81, which runs with MTP off. **[Inference]** B86's failure is exactly the pre-PR behavior, so our pinned build almost certainly predates the fix. Using fp8 KV requires a newer vLLM, not a flag change.

The honest value of fp8 KV is smaller than its headline. **[Inference]** B87 already bought the same capacity in bf16 by raising the KV budget from 7 to 12 GiB. That took the pool from 263,568 to 452,340 tokens, running sequences from 10 to 17, and generation up 21%, with levels flat at 18/18/18. The verdict was "KV is no longer the binder, decode compute is" ([Thui MAP B87](https://github.com/Sahasawatt/arc-agi-3-agent/blob/master/notes/wayfinder/MAP.md)). B87 was a single 3-game, 30-minute smoke, so it is weak evidence. A later B100 log still showed the queue KV-bound at 95% usage. So fp8 KV adds something new only beyond 12 GiB: about 800K tokens, enough to run all 28 sequences with no queue, or to raise concurrency to about 37. At 37 concurrent games the 110 hidden games fit in 3 waves instead of 4, which raises each game's clock from 7,920 s to about 10,800 s within the 9-hour limit. Thuitanium derives the per-game clock from exactly this wave arithmetic ([Thui run.py](https://github.com/Sahasawatt/arc-agi-3-agent/blob/master/localrig/ARC3-Inference/inference/framework/run.py)). The combined "fp8 KV + 3-wave" arm is the one novel, cheap, engine-level experiment available to a Kaggle-only team. I expect **+0 to +0.4 hidden**, because more clock and more concurrency have historically converted into actions rather than levels.

Getting there on Kaggle takes three checks, in cost order. First, read the PR #55557 diff. If it is Python-level (backend dispatch plus Triton kernels), overlay the changed files onto the existing wheel in a Kaggle dataset. If it touches compiled CUDA code, a from-source sm_120 build is needed, which is a multi-hour CPU job and a real risk. Second, check whether tantan's public nightly wheelhouse (vLLM 0.26.1rc1 with DFlash2/MTP) was built after Sep 16 and loads `qwen4_exp` with PLE offload ([tantan graft inventory §D](https://github.com/tantan0327/arc-agi-3-agent/blob/main/docs/graft-inventory.md)). If so, the fix may already be packaged. Third, boot with `--kv-cache-dtype fp8_e4m3` and confirm that the reported KV token count rises about 1.77× without the QSA error. B97 showed a trap to watch for: `serving_setup.py` verifies its own sha256, and one constant drives both `--max-model-len` and the analyzer window. Configuration changes must therefore be made through a rebuilt file, not in-place patches ([Thui MAP B97](https://github.com/Sahasawatt/arc-agi-3-agent/blob/master/notes/wayfinder/MAP.md)).

| **Final top 5** | **~1%** | Needs 18–20%. No Kaggle-only lever has hidden evidence above ~+1 point; the likely source of the gap (post-training Flash-Next) is ruled out by our compute decision |
| Paper Track top 3 ($50K / $20K / $5K) | **~3–5%** | The code need not score well, but we compete with teams holding far stronger results |
| Paper Track outstanding-paper bonus (score > 4.5/5) | **~1–2%** | Six equally weighted 0–5 criteria: Accuracy, Universality, Progress, Theory, Completeness and Novelty ([Kaggle Paper Track](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-paper-track)) |

The Paper Track is a separate Kaggle competition. It asks for a writeup of at most 1,500 words with an attached public notebook. The team must match a team that made an ARC-AGI-2 or ARC-AGI-3 code submission, and the prizes are **$50K / $20K / $5K plus a $375K pool** for papers scoring above 4.5/5 ([Kaggle Paper Track](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-paper-track); [arcprize.org paper track](https://arcprize.org/competitions/2026/paper)). The code "does not need to score well", which removes our biggest handicap. The strongest paper we can write turns our constraint into the contribution. **[Inference]** The paper would be a pre-registered study of what a single Kaggle GPU can learn about ARC-AGI-3. Its data would be our own ~35 alternating hidden draws, which is a rare randomized hidden-set dataset, since most teams submit whatever they built that day. It would combine them with measured variance, the public-to-hidden shrink (about 2–4×, with the offline-to-board r = −0.39), the power arithmetic that limits every team, and the fp8/3-wave result either way. That scores on Accuracy and Completeness, and a clean statistical-methods angle can claim Theory. It will be weak on Progress unless the scores move. The October schedule therefore already doubles as the paper's experiment. The alternation above should be kept for that reason even when it costs a lottery ticket on the leaderboard.

## Conclusion

The gap between 4% and 16% is no longer mysterious in kind, only in detail. The teams above it almost certainly run the same Flash-Next engine we do, then add something they can buy and we cannot: an off-Kaggle node to post-train the model, or enough compute to run hundreds of offline evaluations per week. Choosing Kaggle GPUs only is therefore not a neutral constraint. It removes the most likely mechanism behind the top scores, and it caps the testable levers at the one or two that a daily submission budget can resolve. The rational response is to stop treating the leaderboard as the main prize path. Leave a disciplined, alternating hidden-draw campaign running on it, and bet on the few asymmetric events: a strong notebook going public on Sep 30, the fp8/3-wave engine test, and a team merge.

The fp8 episode carries the wider lesson. A "failed" lever in one team's ledger and a "working" lever in another's fork were never measuring the same thing. With undisclosed top methods, many of the community's closed nulls are really statements about one pinned build. The study our constraints make possible, one that treats Kaggle's hidden set as a noisy instrument and reports what it can and cannot resolve, is also the work most likely to earn prize money for this team.
